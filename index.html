The thesis

A name that owns the financing layer.

Mining gets the headlines. Financing gets the return. A royalty company pays a miner once, up front, and then collects a percentage of what comes out of the ground for the life of the mine — no diesel bills, no tailings dams, no cost overruns. It is the toll road of the mining business, and in rare earths that toll road is barely built.

That is the opening this name speaks to. The rare-earth chain is the most strategically watched supply chain on earth: one country mines about 69% of it and refines far more, governments are writing price floors into law-sized deals, and the two producers of scale outside China cannot expand fast enough. Capital is the constraint. A royalty brand sits exactly on that constraint.

The name is direct, pronounceable, and exact. It works on a royalty firm's letterhead, on a streaming deal's term sheet, on a critical-minerals fund's prospectus, and in front of a mine developer who needs to know, in one glance, that this is not a general mining site. It is a royalties site first, and a rare-earths site second — which is precisely the order a financier reads it in.

Specific, and broad enough to travel. A strong rare-earth royalty brand must cover three linked markets at once: the mines that produce the oxides, the magnets that consume them, and the capital that bridges the two. This name covers royalty acquisition, streaming finance, project finance, and critical-minerals investment without narrowing to any single one — and without vagueness that says nothing at all.
