The Stockpile Is the New Buyer

Washington is building a $12 billion mineral stockpile, and the November 10 deadline on China’s export-control pause is the clock ticking over it.

By Prime Domain Capital ResearchNovember 9, 20266-minute read

On February 2, 2026, the president announced Project Vault in the Oval Office: a $12 billion public-private initiative to build a strategic stockpile of critical minerals, backed by a $10 billion direct loan from the Export-Import Bank of the United States and about $2 billion in private capital. The model is the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, extended from oil to the minerals that go into motors, turbines, and weapons systems. The stated purpose is a 60-day emergency supply for American manufacturers. The unstated purpose is a buyer that does not flinch when Beijing tightens the taps.

The timing is not decorative. China’s October 2025 package of rare-earth controls was suspended on November 7, 2025, as part of a trade truce, with the suspension running to November 10, 2026. The broader truce was extended in late September to January 10, 2027, but no formal notice naming rare earths had been published by early November, and the legal suspension ends on November 10 without one. The April 2025 controls on the original seven elements — samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium, and yttrium — were never suspended at all. Project Vault was announced into a window that closes on a specific date, and that date is now days away.

01 — The chequebook behind the stockpile

Project Vault is the largest single item, but it is not the only one. Over the past year the Export-Import Bank has issued $14.8 billion in letters of interest for critical-minerals projects, including $455 million for rare-earth development and processing in the United States, $400 million for lithium extraction in Arkansas, $350 million for cobalt and nickel production in Australia, and $215 million for tin extraction in the United Kingdom and Australia. Its authorized portfolio also includes $1.3 billion for the Reko Diq copper and gold project in Pakistan. The $10 billion Vault loan alone is more than double the largest financing in the bank’s history. A stockpile is a purchase order written by a government, and this one is written at a scale the market has never seen from a single buyer.

The supply side is moving just as fast. MP Materials reported third-quarter 2026 results on November 5: NdPr production of 1,018 metric tons, up 41% year over year, and NdPr sales of 1,020 tons, up 94%. The company recognized $8.1 million of price-protection income under its Pentagon agreement, with revenue plus that income totaling $155.8 million. Its heavy rare-earth separation — the part of the chain that matters for dysprosium and terbium — is set to begin commercial production in the fourth quarter of 2026, with saleable magnets shipping to General Motors in the same quarter and Apple deliveries starting in 2027 under a partnership worth more than $500 million, backed by a $40 million Apple prepayment. In September, MP acquired Brazil’s Serra Verde Group, the only operation outside Asia producing all four critical magnetic rare-earth elements. USA Rare Earth, meanwhile, was awarded up to $1.6 billion in CHIPS Act funding to build its mine-to-magnet chain. China controls about 70% of rare-earth extraction and 90% of processing. The Vault’s sourcing plan — roughly 70% domestic, 30% from allied partners — is a direct answer to those two numbers.

A stockpile is a purchase order written by a government, and this one is written at a scale the market has never seen from a single buyer.

02 — The honest caveat

There are good reasons not to treat the stockpile as a price floor for the whole market. It buys roughly sixty days of supply, not a market. Prices are already softening: the NdPr alloy benchmark fell 4.75% to $127.10 a kilogram in the November 2026 outlook, and MP Materials’ shares are down about 62% from their high, falling 11.9% in October after a 13.6% drop in September. Elevated heavy rare-earth prices, driven by supply constraints, are not expected to last indefinitely — the premium itself finances the mines and separation plants that narrow it. And a buyer this large becomes the price: a stockpile that buys at the top can turn into the seller that caps the market when it stops buying, or starts releasing.

But the direction of travel is set by the structure, not the headlines. The Pentagon’s $110-a-kilogram NdPr floor already pays MP Materials when the benchmark falls below it. Project Vault adds a second government buyer, this one for physical material rather than price support. A producer outside China no longer has to win on cost against a refiner that controls nine-tenths of the chain. It has to win a purchase order from a buyer whose reason for buying is that the chain exists at all. For a royalty on that producer’s output, that is the difference between a claim on a commodity price and a claim on a volume that a government has committed to buy. The stockpile does not make rare earths safe. It makes the demand for ex-China supply something a royalty holder can underwrite.
